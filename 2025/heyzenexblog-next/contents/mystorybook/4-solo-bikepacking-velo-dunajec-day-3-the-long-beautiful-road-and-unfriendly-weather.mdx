I started riding from Nowy Sącz around 7:30 in the morning, when the air was
still cool and the town was just beginning to wake up. The first few kilometers
felt almost effortless — smooth roads, quiet surroundings, and that calm energy
you only get early in the day. For a moment, I thought this would be an easy
ride.

But that didn’t last long.

Soon, the climbs began. And they didn’t stop. It was just up and up and up — long, steady ascents that tested both my legs and my patience.
Each turn revealed another hill, another stretch of road rising in front of me. It was exhausting, no doubt about it. Still, somewhere in the back of my mind, I kept telling myself it would be worth it.
There had to be something waiting at the top.

And there was.

<PhoCol photo={viewFromTheTop} />

When I finally reached the higher point, the view opened up in a way I didn’t expect. In front of me was a massive lake, perfectly framed by mountains, like something out of a painting. It felt almost unreal — like a quiet reward for every drop of sweat I had put into those climbs. I stopped for a moment just to take it in.

As I started descending toward the lake, the scenery kept getting better. The closer I got, the more details I noticed — the reflections on the water, the layers of mountains in the distance, the silence of the place. It’s hard to describe exactly how I felt in that moment. A mix of peace, happiness, and something deeper — maybe gratitude. Being there alone, surrounded by that kind of beauty, made it even more special.

Also, I had to cross an old bridge. And honestly, that was one of the scariest moments of the entire trip. The structure didn’t feel completely stable, and as I rode across it, I could feel it moving beneath me — swaying slightly with the wind and the weight of my bike. For a few seconds, my focus shifted from enjoying the journey to just getting across safely. Somehow, I made it without any trouble, but my heart was definitely racing by the time I reached the other side.

After that, the landscape changed. The dramatic views slowly gave way to long, straight roads stretching through open fields and patches of forest. It was still nice, but in a quieter, more repetitive way. What made it harder was the heat. The sun was relentless, and the temperature kept rising with every kilometer. It turned the ride into more of a mental challenge than a physical one.

<PhoCol photo={releentlessRoad} />

At one point, I think 20 km before reaching Tarnow, I came across a house offering free drinks for cyclists, which turned out to be perfectly timed — I had just run out of water. The drink tasted a bit like honey, with a strong, distinctive flavor, but it was incredibly energizing and exactly what I needed to keep going.

<PhoCol photo={energizingDrink} />

By the time I reached Tarnów around 3 p.m., I was completely drained. I was close to my planned campsite, but the idea of setting up a tent in that heat just didn’t make sense anymore. I needed rest, shade, and something cooler than a fabric shelter under the sun. So I made a quick decision — I booked a hotel, took a proper shower, and allowed myself to finally relax.

And just like that, I had reached the end of the Velo Dunajec route.

Tired, a bit sunburned, but I was happy :).
